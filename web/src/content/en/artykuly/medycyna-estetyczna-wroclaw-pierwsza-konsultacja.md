*This article is informational and educational. It does not replace a medical consultation, and the decision about any treatment is made by the physician together with the patient after taking a history and examining them.*

I perform aesthetic medicine treatments in Wrocław. The question I hear most often from interested people is: “Where do I start?” The answer is always the same: with a conversation with the physician, not with choosing a specific treatment.

## What a consultation is

A consultation is a meeting at which the physician takes a history, assesses the condition of the skin and tissues and discusses which methods may be appropriate and which are not. A well-conducted conversation also clarifies what a treatment can and cannot do. No treatment guarantees a result, and the body's response differs from person to person.

## How to prepare

- **Prepare a list of medications and supplements**, including those taken occasionally, as well as information about chronic conditions and allergies.
- **Gather information about previous treatments**: what was done, when and with what. This matters especially if products are already present in the tissues.
- **Write down what concerns you** and what you expect. A specific description of the problem makes it easier to choose a method.
- **Mention pregnancy, breastfeeding** or planned pregnancy, as well as infections, including cold sores.

## What to ask the physician

1. What are the indications and contraindications for the proposed method?
2. What are the alternatives, including no treatment?
3. What are the possible side effects and what should I do if they occur?
4. What does the period after the treatment look like and when can I return to daily activities?
5. Which product or device will be used and on what basis was it chosen?

## Qualification and safety

Proper qualification includes a history, an examination and a discussion of risks. Sometimes after a consultation the physician advises against a treatment or suggests postponing it. This is a normal part of responsible practice, not a refusal to help.

## Combining the perspectives of an orthodontist and aesthetic medicine

As a dentist I also work in orthodontics, so I look at facial aesthetics as a whole: I take into account the bite, facial proportions and muscle tension. This perspective can help with planning, but it does not mean every problem needs a treatment.
